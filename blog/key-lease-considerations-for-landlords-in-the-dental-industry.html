Key Lease Considerations for Landlords in the Dental Industry

By Cohen Law Firm Editorial Team. Published January 2, 2025, updated May 25, 2026.

Leasing to dental practices presents unique opportunities and challenges for commercial property owners. Understanding these specifics helps maximize property value while maintaining strong tenant relationships. As the dental industry continues to evolve, landlords who grasp the nuances of dental leasing can create more valuable, stable properties.

Here are three important tips for landlords when leasing to dentists.

1. Understand Lease Economics and Market Value

The structure of your dental office lease directly impacts your property's market value, particularly when dealing with Dental Support Organizations (DSOs). A well-structured lease should align with current market rates while accounting for the specialized nature of dental spaces. This becomes especially important when working with DSOs, as their approach to lease valuation often differs from traditional dental practices.

Property owners should recognize that dental tenants typically make substantial investments in their spaces. These improvements, which often include specialized plumbing, electrical systems, and structural modifications, can significantly enhance property value. Understanding this dynamic helps inform lease term decisions and improvement allowances that benefit both parties.

2. Reserve Consent on Lease Assignments

While maintaining control over your property is crucial, overly restrictive assignment provisions can deter quality dental tenants. A balanced approach to assignment rights protects your interests while making your property more attractive to dental tenants planning long-term occupancy. Consider implementing reasonable consent rights for practice transitions, establishing clear financial criteria for potential assignees, and creating streamlined approval processes for qualified buyers.

The key is to maintain property standards while facilitating practice sales. This approach recognizes that most dental practices will eventually transition ownership, and making this process smoother increases your property's appeal to dental tenants. Your lease should outline specific, objective criteria for evaluating potential assignees, focusing on financial stability and professional credentials rather than subjective factors.

3. Determine Lease Terms Carefully

Lease duration significantly impacts both property stability and value. Longer terms, typically ranging from seven to ten years, often appeal to practice owners seeking stability and banks providing practice financing. However, in high-demand markets, shorter terms might maximize flexibility and potential returns. Understanding your market position and long-term investment goals should guide your approach to lease duration.

Consider incorporating strategic renewal options that protect both parties' interests. These options should include predetermined rent increases that maintain market alignment while providing tenants with predictability for their business planning. This approach helps create sustainable, long-term tenancy relationships that enhance property value.

Conclusion

Leasing to dental professionals can be a valuable opportunity for landlords, but it requires careful planning and market awareness. By understanding the economic impact of lease terms, retaining consent on assignments, and strategically determining lease lengths, landlords can maximize property value and ensure successful tenant relationships. These considerations will set the stage for a productive, long-term partnership with dental tenants.
