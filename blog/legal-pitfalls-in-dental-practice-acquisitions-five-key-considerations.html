Legal Pitfalls in Dental Practice Acquisitions: Five Key Considerations

By Cohen Law Firm Editorial Team. Published August 13, 2024, updated May 25, 2026.

Acquiring a dental practice is a significant step in a dentist's career, but the process is fraught with potential legal pitfalls that can have long-lasting consequences. Here are five crucial considerations to keep in mind:

1. The Dangers of Going Solo

Dentists are experts in oral health, not law, insurance, or accountancy. Yet, all these areas of expertise come into play during a dental practice acquisition. Attempting to navigate this complex process alone can lead to costly mistakes. While it may seem expensive initially, investing in professional expertise early in the process can save you from significant financial and legal headaches in the long run. Consider hiring a healthcare attorney specializing in dental practices, an accountant familiar with dental practice finances, and an insurance expert to review and advise on coverage. The cost of this professional advice is an investment in your future and is far less expensive than dealing with the consequences of a poorly structured deal.

2. The Importance of Assignment Clauses in Non-Compete Agreements

Non-compete agreements are standard in dental practice acquisitions. They prevent the selling dentist from immediately setting up a competing practice nearby. However, these agreements need careful consideration. Ensure your non-compete agreement includes an assignment clause that stipulates the non-compete does not transfer to a new owner if you decide to sell the practice in the future. Without this clause, you could find yourself unable to sell your practice down the line, as potential buyers might be deterred by an overly restrictive non-compete agreement. This seemingly small detail can have a significant impact on your ability to make future career decisions.

3. Securing Vital Representations and Guarantees

To protect yourself from future legal issues, it's crucial to get all necessary assurances in writing. Work with your attorney to create a comprehensive list of representations and warranties tailored to your specific situation. Essential guarantees to obtain include clear title to all assets being sold, accuracy and completeness of financial documentation, compliance with all relevant laws and regulations, and disclosure of any pending legal actions or disputes. These written assurances provide a safety net, allowing you to proceed with confidence and giving you recourse if issues arise after the sale is complete.

4. Clarifying the Seller's Future Role

Whether the selling dentist will continue working in the practice post-sale is a critical point that needs early clarification. This decision can significantly impact patient retention, practice valuation, transition planning, and future staffing needs. Address this issue at the beginning of negotiations. Don't invest time and money into a deal without agreeing on this potentially make-or-break point. A clear understanding of the seller's future involvement can help you plan for a smooth transition and set realistic expectations for the practice's future performance.

5. Including Tax Allocation in the Purchase Agreement

The allocation of the purchase price across different asset categories can have significant tax implications for both buyer and seller. The purchase price is typically broken down into categories such as furniture and equipment, patient records and goodwill, accounts receivable, and real estate (if applicable). This allocation can affect the seller's tax liability, the buyer's ability to depreciate certain assets, and the overall financial structure of the deal. Work with your accountant and attorney to structure the allocation in a way that's beneficial for both parties while complying with IRS regulations. A well-planned tax allocation can result in substantial savings and prevent disputes with tax authorities down the line.

Conclusion

Acquiring a dental practice is a complex process with numerous legal considerations. By being aware of these potential pitfalls and seeking professional guidance, you can navigate the acquisition process more smoothly and set yourself up for long-term success in your new practice. Remember, thorough preparation and expert advice are key to ensuring a successful transition and protecting your investment in your professional future.
