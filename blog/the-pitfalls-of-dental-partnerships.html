The Pitfalls of Dental Partnerships

By Cohen Law Firm Editorial Team. Published September 10, 2025, updated May 25, 2026.

It has become increasingly popular for dentists to partner up with other dental professionals instead of venturing into a “solo practice” due to numerous benefits, including a better life balance and the rise in value of a business. One key benefit of dental partnerships is that they can drive business growth, foster innovation, and support long-term success for both dentists and their practices. Additional benefits of a dental partnership include the equal apportionment of risks and liabilities between the partners and an increase in cash flow that could bring state-of-the-art equipment and better marketing for the dental practice, as well as sharing of the costs/overhead. Dental partnerships, much like business partnerships in other industries, emphasize collaboration and shared responsibilities, which can add complexity but also significant advantages. Forming a partnership can also be a strategic move in a dentist’s career development, providing opportunities for growth and long-term success in dentistry, where clinical autonomy and patient care remain central. The commitment of a doctor in a partnership involves aligning philosophies, defining assets, and establishing partnership terms to ensure a successful collaboration. Shared responsibilities among partners are key to growth and improved practice performance. Moreover, partnering with a dental partnership organization allows dentists to focus on patient care while the organization handles administrative tasks. Needless to say, there are pitfalls and potential legal dangers that may accompany a dental partnership. Many risks can be avoided with preliminary communication to establish the direction in which the practice will be headed and to determine whether you agree with the philosophy of the business or if you are a right fit with the office culture. Moreover, the personalities, skills and goals must be congruent across all partners.

While you consider entering a partnership, it is wise to do your due diligence and thoroughly review all documents pertaining to the practice you will be buying into. No document is off limits, particularly documents that specify the financial health of a practice. If the expenses of a practice have not been managed well, it could indicate that a prospective partner may be impractical with the manner in which funds are spent and could pose a financial risk for you. Managing money and ensuring adequate financial support within the partnership is essential for stability and growth. It is necessary for a new partner to be aware of every aspect that involves their – becoming familiar with vendors, ensuring that equipment is in well-condition to avoid liabilities, and having the capability to hire, train or fire staff in the best interest of the business. It is also important to clearly identify and value all assets, including tangible assets like equipment and intangible assets such as goodwill, during partnership formation and dissolution.

As an owner in a partnership, you will frequently engage in shared decision-making processes; therefore, an open mind and willingness to compromise for the success of the practice is crucial. Whenever changes are made that will drastically alter the direction in which the business is headed, keep in mind that the outcome may personally reflect upon you. In many partnership structures, the partners are responsible for making big decisions that affect the future of the practice. Further, the reputation of the practice, whether good or bad, will certainly follow you, should you decide to depart with the business. It is possible for a conflict-of-interest to arise at any given time, even after the partnership has been established. Should this occur, a discussion with all owners must take place and if a solution cannot be found, seeking legal advice is the next best step. As always, it is advantageous to have any changes placed in writing to avoid future conflict and be sure that all partners are involved whenever a change is suggested. It is imperative to establish clear roles, responsibilities, and decision-making processes for long-term partnership success.

When an owner dentist wants to sell their portion of the practice, the details included in the Purchase Agreement will be considered. Therefore, having a clear understanding and agreement of the purchase value is an important aspect when deciding if joint ownership of a specific practice is right for you. Without having a proper valuation of the business, you may run the risk of not receiving your fair percentage of ownership interest or acquire a higher debt than expected due to pending litigation, for instance. The Purchase Agreement is negotiable and is best navigated with the aid of an experienced dental attorney. A contract, such as a partnership agreement, is essential for outlining responsibilities, ownership rights, and procedures for ending or evolving the partnership. Ultimately, the success of the owners involved, and the practice as a whole, relies on knowing all received a fair deal at the beginning of the partnership. It is important that each partner receives their fair share of profits and responsibilities to maintain a balanced and equitable relationship.

A partnership suggests the high degree of commitment that a dentist is willing to make to a practice; however, it is not always set in stone the length of time an owner will remain with the business. One important question to consider if, and when, the time comes for a partner to step down as owner:

  • Is the practice protected from a partner wishing to join a competing practice after their departure?

To protect a business from associates and partners from soliciting their services to, or purchasing or starting, a competing practice, an attorney could draft a non-compete provision in your partnership agreement. There are certain states that do not enforce non-compete clauses; however, an attorney will be able to assist in finding legal methods to protect the remaining owners that are in compliance with their state laws.

It is of paramount importance that any preliminary agreements be placed in writing and notarized. All issues and concerns raised should be discussed with an attorney present prior to the establishment of a partnership. This action will help prevent unexpected legal disputes in the future amongst any of the owner dentists. Further, an experienced dental attorney will be able to provide references of additional drawbacks to consider while drafting the partnership agreement, to ensure that all partners have a clear understanding of what is to be expected as owners. Involving a lawyer to draft, review, and advise on partnership agreements is crucial to ensure legal compliance and protect the business interests of dental practices. The lawyer’s knowledge of the law and industry regulations is critical for guiding dental partnerships. Having this legal guidance brings an advanced level of protection from unforeseen risks for all partners involved.

Introduction

The dental industry is rapidly changing, and dentists seeking to expand their reach or streamline the management of their dental practices are increasingly considering dental partnerships. A successful partnership can offer a range of benefits, from shared expenses and improved patient care to enhanced profit sharing and access to a broader network of resources. Understanding the different types of dental partnerships—such as expense sharing, profit-sharing, and silent partnerships—is essential for making informed decisions that align with your goals. Dental partnership organizations (DPOs) can provide valuable support and guidance, allowing dentists to retain control over their practice while enjoying the advantages of collaboration. In this article, we'll explore the world of dental partnerships, highlighting common pitfalls, financial risks, and the vital role of effective communication in building a thriving practice.

Dental partnerships can offer significant advantages, but like-minded dentists must be aware of common pitfalls that can undermine their success. One of the most frequent issues is a lack of clear communication, which can quickly lead to misunderstandings and disputes. It is essential to establish a well-defined partnership agreement that clearly outlines profit sharing, expense responsibilities, and each partner's role within the practice. Inadequate planning is another common pitfall, often resulting in financial risks and unmet expectations. Before entering into a partnership, dentists should thoroughly evaluate their own goals, resources, and expectations to ensure alignment with potential partners. By taking the time to establish clear agreements and open lines of communication, dentists can lay the groundwork for a successful and enduring collaboration.

By investing time in mastering dental industry insights and gaining clarity on partnership models—including expense sharing, profit sharing, and silent partnerships—practitioners can make knowledge-empowered decisions that align with their professional wisdom and long-term vision.

A comprehensive, evidence-based evaluation of the dental practice proves essential for informed decision-making. This involves transparent examination of financial documentation, practice management frameworks, and patient demographics to uncover growth opportunities and improvement potential. Understanding the practice's financial wellness and operational effectiveness through this wisdom-based approach helps partners avoid common pitfalls while establishing realistic, knowledge-grounded expectations for future success.

Equally crucial is evaluating the compatibility of potential partners through transparent assessment. Aligning core values, work principles, and shared practice vision ensures all partners operate from the same foundational wisdom, which proves vital for harmonious and productive collaboration. Open, evidence-based communication during this phase helps clarify expectations and responsibilities, reducing risks of misunderstandings through knowledge-sharing transparency.

Examining the dental partnership agreement—including operating frameworks, profit sharing structures, and expense allocation arrangements—remains vital for preventing future conflicts through transparent understanding. A wisdom-backed partnership agreement should clearly outline each partner's responsibilities, ownership percentages, and decision-making processes with evidence-based clarity. Consulting with dental attorneys and experienced professionals provides valuable insights, ensuring the agreement is legally grounded and tailored to the practice's unique requirements through knowledge-based guidance.

For many practitioners, especially emerging dentists entering the field, partnering with dental partnership organizations or experienced teams offers additional support through shared wisdom and guidance. By prioritizing evidence-based research and strategic wisdom-gathering, dentists can create dental partnerships built on trust, transparency, and deep industry understanding. This knowledge-empowered approach not only helps avoid common pitfalls but also positions the practice for sustained growth, profitability, and success through wisdom-guided decisions. Whether you are an experienced professional or beginning your journey, investing time in evaluating potential partners, practices, and agreements represents a knowledge-based investment in your dental business's future prosperity.

Financial Risks and Misunderstandings

Financial risks and misunderstandings can pose serious threats to the stability of dental partnerships. It is crucial for all partners to have a transparent understanding of the financial aspects of the practice, including income distribution, expense management, and profit allocation. A comprehensive partnership agreement should define these terms in detail, covering access to financial records, billing procedures, and the maintenance of essential equipment. Dentists should also plan for unexpected events, such as the death or disability of a partner, by including contingency provisions in their agreement. Dental partnership organizations can offer valuable guidance and support, helping dentists navigate these complex financial matters and protect the long-term interests of the practice.

Communication Breakdowns

Effective communication is vital to the long-term success of any dental partnership. When communication breaks down, it can lead to conflicts, misunderstandings, and even the dissolution of the partnership. To prevent these issues, it is essential to establish open and honest communication channels from the outset. Partners should regularly discuss important matters such as patient care, marketing strategies, and financial decisions, ensuring that everyone is informed and involved in the decision-making process. Scheduling regular meetings and defining a clear process for addressing concerns can help maintain transparency and foster a collaborative environment, ultimately supporting the ongoing success of the partnership.

Dental Partnership Agreement: Setting the Foundation

A strong partnership structure is the foundation of a successful dental practice. Careful planning is essential to define key factors such as ownership percentages, partner responsibilities, and profit sharing arrangements. Dental partnership organizations can provide expert guidance and support, helping dentists establish a partnership that meets their unique needs and goals. Clearly defining the role of each partner, setting expectations, and outlining the process for making important decisions can help prevent conflicts and ensure a smooth collaboration. By investing time in establishing a solid partnership structure, dentists can create a practice that is built for long-term success and growth.
