Legal Considerations Every Dentist Should Know Before Signing a Lease

By Dhruva William, Marketing. Published May 5, 2025, updated May 25, 2026.

When it comes to establishing a successful dental practice, choosing the right location is a critical factor. However, securing a lease for that ideal space involves careful consideration and understanding of various legal aspects. At Cohen Law Firm PLLC, we recognise that navigating lease agreements can be complex, especially for dental professionals. Here are essential legal considerations every dentist should keep in mind before signing a lease, ensuring that your practice is set up for success from the start.

Understanding Lease Types

Before exploring the details of a lease agreement, it's essential to understand the various types of leases. The most common structures include gross leases, net leases, and modified gross leases—each assigning different responsibilities for rent and operating expenses.

In a gross lease, the landlord usually handles all operating costs, such as property taxes, maintenance, and insurance. This can simplify budgeting for dental practices, as the monthly payment remains consistent. On the other hand, a net lease requires the tenant to cover some or all property-related expenses, potentially causing variable costs. Recognising these differences is essential, as they can greatly affect your practice’s financial planning.

Negotiating Terms and Conditions

Once you have identified the type of lease that suits your needs, the next step is negotiation. The terms and conditions outlined in a lease can greatly affect your practice's operations, so it’s essential to approach this process with care. Key elements to negotiate include the length of the lease, renewal options, rent increases, and the inclusion of tenant improvements.

As dental lawyers, we emphasise the importance of having the right to renew your lease. A favourable renewal option can provide stability for your practice, allowing you to maintain your location without the uncertainty of relocation. Additionally, being proactive about potential rent increases can help prevent financial strain in the future.

Understanding Zoning Regulations

Zoning regulations play a significant role in the operation of dental practices. Before finalising a lease, confirm that the property is properly zoned to allow dental practice operations. Zoning laws dictate what types of businesses can operate in specific areas, and non-compliance can lead to legal issues down the road.

As part of our commitment to serving dental professionals, we recommend conducting thorough due diligence regarding zoning regulations. This may involve consulting with local zoning authorities or hiring a knowledgeable real estate attorney to assist in the process. Ensuring that your practice operates within the legal framework can save you from potential headaches in the future.

Reviewing Your Responsibilities

It's essential to understand your obligations as a tenant before signing a lease. Lease agreements often outline specific responsibilities regarding maintenance, repairs, and utilities. As dental lawyers, we advise our clients to carefully review these clauses to avoid unexpected costs or disputes later on.

Consider the implications of maintenance responsibilities. For example, if you are responsible for significant repairs or upkeep of the property, this could impact your budget and overall profitability. We encourage dentists to negotiate terms that clearly define responsibilities, ensuring that both parties are on the same page.

Assessing the Condition of the Property

Before committing to a lease, conducting a thorough inspection of the property is crucial. This assessment should include the overall condition of the space, any necessary repairs, and compliance with health and safety codes.

A comprehensive property inspection can reveal potential issues that may not be immediately apparent. For instance, plumbing, electrical systems, and HVAC units should be evaluated to ensure they meet the needs of a dental practice. Identifying these factors early on can help avoid costly repairs after you have signed the lease.

Understanding Exit Strategies

While we all hope for the best, it’s essential to plan for the unexpected. An exit strategy outlines the steps to take if you need to terminate the lease before its expiration. Understanding the terms related to lease termination, including penalties and notice periods, can provide peace of mind.

At Cohen Law Firm PLLC, we work with dental professionals to establish clear exit strategies in their lease agreements. This foresight can be invaluable if unexpected circumstances arise, such as changes in business direction or economic conditions.

Working with Experienced Dental Lawyers

Navigating lease agreements can be daunting, especially for dental professionals who may not have a legal background. Partnering with experienced dental lawyers can provide invaluable support throughout the leasing process. At Cohen Law Firm, we specialise in representing dentists and dental specialists, ensuring that their interests are protected in every transaction.

Our team can assist in reviewing lease agreements, negotiating favourable terms, and ensuring compliance with local regulationsWith our expertise supporting you, you can concentrate on delivering outstanding dental care to your patients.

Final Thoughts

Signing a lease is a significant step in establishing your dental practice. Taking these legal factors into account allows you to make well-informed choices that contribute to your business’s long-term success. As dental lawyers dedicated to serving the unique needs of dental professionals, we are here to guide you through the complexities of lease agreements, ensuring you enter into arrangements that foster growth and stability for your practice.

If you are ready to take the next step in securing your dental practice's future, reach out to Cohen Law Firm PLLC. Together, we can navigate the legal landscape and help you achieve your professional goals with confidence.
