What is a dental practice?

A successful dental practice acquisition refers to the professional practice of a dentist, typically providing dental services such as preventive care, fillings, crowns, root canals, and other treatments to maintain or improve oral health. For example, a typical dental practice may offer routine cleanings, cosmetic procedures, and emergency dental care to a diverse patient base.

It is important for anyone considering dentistry as a career or looking to purchase an existing dental practice to understand how this type of business works. Buying a dental practice involves assessing the value of existing equipment, negotiating with sellers on fair prices, and understanding financing options, including dental practice loans.

Ultimately, it requires due diligence to ensure that the dental practice you are buying has the potential for growth and success. It is also essential to conduct thorough research before diving into any medical practice acquisition. Ensure you have access to the practice's financial records and evaluate revenue streams and expenses.

Things to consider before buying a dental practice?

The following are the key things to consider before buying a dental practice:

  • Conduct thorough research on the practice you are considering, including financial records, revenue streams, and expenses.
  • Engage a dental practice broker to find suitable opportunities and conduct market research.
  • Evaluate the value of existing equipment and negotiate with the selling dentist for a fair price.
  • Understand financing options available and secure third-party funding if necessary.
  • Assess the potential for growth and success in the dental practice you’re buying.
  • Investigate any potential legal or regulatory issues associated with purchasing the practice.
  • Put a financial agreement in place to avoid potential disagreements down the line.
  • Consider both practical and emotional factors when making an offer to purchase.
  • Seek help when negotiating real estate purchases or leases early in the process.
  • Understand all terms of any contract before signing.
  • Consider your practice goals and how the purchase aligns with your long-term objectives and strategic planning.
  • Evaluate your specific situation, as different business structures or transition approaches may be more appropriate depending on your unique circumstances.
  • Lastly, creating a business plan when buying any dental practice is important to ensure you understand the risks associated with owning and operating such a business.

Finding the Right Dental Office

Finding the right dental office is a key factor in a successful dental practice acquisition. Start by considering the location—proximity to your target patient base, accessibility, and visibility can all impact patient flow and the long-term growth of your practice. Evaluate the existing patient base to ensure it aligns with your personal financial goals and professional vision. A strong, loyal patient base and steady patient flow are indicators of a healthy, established practice.

Next, assess the dental office’s financial performance by reviewing recent revenue, expenses, and profitability. This will help you determine if the practice can support your desired income and future growth. Don’t overlook the condition and quality of the equipment, including dental chairs and technology. Up-to-date equipment not only improves efficiency but also enhances patient care and satisfaction.

Finally, consider how the practice’s culture, staff, and reputation fit with your own values and goals. By thoroughly analyzing these factors, you can confidently select a dental office that supports your ambitions and sets the stage for a thriving dental practice.

What are the pros of Buying a Dental Practice?

Many dentists opt to buy existing practices because it offers immediate access to a patient base, trained staff, and established systems. Owning your own dental practice by buying an already-established one can be a great way to start a business that does well. The main benefit of purchasing a dental practice is that it has a patient base and staff who are already trained and ready to go.

Additionally, with the current financials of US dental practices, you can get a good idea of what to expect in terms of profits. The current practice may have invested in digital marketing and seo, as well as traditional brick and mortar marketing and you save on those costs because you already have patients.

Are there any drawbacks to buying a Dental practice?

It’s not all sunshine and roses when looking to buy a dental practice. There are some risks associated with making such a purchase that should be taken into consideration before moving ahead.

One major risk is acquiring the wrong practice, which can lead to costly mistakes, operational challenges, and long-term financial setbacks. The difference between a successful and unsuccessful acquisition often comes down to careful evaluation and understanding of what sets a thriving practice apart from one with hidden issues.

These include taking on the previous practice owner debts, knowing the rules and regulations for running a dental office in your state or region, and any liabilities that come with taking over a practice that has been around for a long time.

A thorough diligence process is essential to evaluate the dental practice seller, establish a valuation, and review aspects like financial performance, patient mix, and office operations. Also, buying an already-established approach could mean updating equipment or systems to stay competitive, which could require more money.

How to buy a dental practice: Eight steps to follow.

Buying a dental practice can be exciting, stressful, and even more mysterious if you want to pursue a dental practice purchase of one already up and running. For this decision-making process to go well and produce excellent results, you must pay attention to a few things, including assembling a team of professional advisors—such as attorneys, accountants, and consultants—to guide you through the legal, financial, and operational aspects.

Down below, we have discussed eight essential tips dentists should consider before purchasing their new workplace, so let’s take another read through them together, shall we?

Early intervention is key.

It is crucial to seek help early on in purchasing a dental practice. To find the right practice for sale, talk to practice brokers, companies selling dental supplies, accountants specializing in healthcare, and small business banking experts. These industry experts can provide valuable guidance throughout the complex process of dental practice acquisition.

These professionals can tell you important things about dentists getting close to retirement age or being willing to add a partner who wants to buy their practice. In addition, it is advisable to sign a non-disclosure agreement to protect both parties’ financial and personal information while avoiding raising any concerns among patients and staff.

Also, it is helpful to hire a buying agent, like a dental practice broker, who can help you through the whole process. If you have friends who are familiar with these kinds of deals, you will be able to get the most up-to-date information about the market and sales.

Budget your expenses.

Creating a budget is essential for anyone planning to purchase a practice. Not only will you need to consider your salary, the loan payment for the method, and any additional expenses such as hiring an associate or other staff, you will also likely need to consider hiring a dental attorney. When evaluating dental practice loans, it's important to consult with banks early in the process to understand the range of available loan products. Carefully review the loan structure of each option, including repayment terms, interest rates, and collateral requirements, to ensure it fits your business needs. Additionally, if you have student debt, be aware that it may impact your financial planning but does not necessarily prevent you from obtaining a practice loan. Dental practice loans, including traditional bank loans, SBA 7(a) loans, and private lender options, should also be evaluated to determine the best financing method.

It can be helpful to determine what size of practice you are looking for and how much income it will have to generate to cover all the costs. This information can be shared with a banker when exploring financial options.

Furthermore, if you plan to have another dentist stay on during the transition, their compensation must be included in the budget. To make sure your budget is accurate, consider taxes and other things that might come up.

You should also consider any investments you might need to make to keep up with technological changes or ensure patients are comfortable and happy. The dental attorney will ensure your peace of mind. Considering all these factors will help you develop an accurate budget so that all needs are met, and you can move forward confidently with purchasing your new dental practice.

Don't forget the value of your existing staff.

When buying a dental practice, it’s essential to know how helpful the current staff is. Not only do they make sure that current patients have a smooth transition, but they also give helpful information about how business systems and operations work. These employees know how the practice works inside and out and have been trained in all aspects of good management.

Assigning the right job roles to professionals, such as attorneys or consultants, during the acquisition process is also crucial to ensure a smooth and successful transition.

Their experience can help you reach your long-term goals for the course in a big way. Moreover, their commitment to customer fee for service practice can help maintain continuity and strong patient relationships. Therefore, when considering a purchase of an existing practice, remember the importance of its current staff; they may be one of the most valuable resources you acquire.

It's worth seeing for yourself.

If you’re considering buying an existing dental practice, more is needed than to just hear about it from a third party. You’ve got to see it for yourself and get an in-person feel for the operation. It will give you essential insight into the patient flow, staff size, cash flow, growth opportunities, and the practice's processes. During your visit, observe and evaluate the processes in place, such as how inventory is managed and how team members integrate into daily workflows, to ensure operational efficiency and smooth practice management. Make sure to take notice of recent figures over the last 18 months to gauge whether the practice is growing, stable, or shrinking.

That way, you’ll be able to identify any untapped opportunities and make an informed decision when purchasing. Take your time, and only rush into a purchase once you’ve seen everything with your own two eyes.

Practices' actual value may differ from the seller's asking price.

Before buying a dental practice, talking to an expert in small business banking is essential. The due diligence process is crucial in evaluating the financial and legal aspects of a potential acquisition. The price the seller asks for the practice may differ from what it is worth. A valuation professional will review financial statements, tax returns, and other records and compare the practice’s finances with national benchmarks.

It is essential to ensure the seller’s asking price accurately reflects the practice’s actual value. This involves an analysis of current gross receipts and expenses, such as staff salaries, rent or mortgage payments, equipment costs, and more. It is also important to consider how certain expenses or loans are paid over time, as these ongoing payments can impact the overall valuation.

A high percentage of accounts receivable or bad debts can be a red flag, as it may indicate potential management issues and negatively affect the practice's value.

It is vital to read the story between these figures, like if staff salaries are taking up too much of gross receipts. Furthermore, looking at data on industry averages can also help determine if the asking price is competitive with similar practices in the practice location.

Start by negotiating the price.

Negotiating the price of practice should always be the first step. By putting a financial agreement in place, both parties can avoid potential disagreements about other parts of the purchase procedure, such as staffing, transition details, pay charges etc.

A third-party professional can make the entire procedure more efficient by assisting with negotiating the price, further ensuring both parties reach an amicable agreement. When making an offer to purchase, consider both practical and emotional factors to ensure a fair deal that works for both sides. Having a financial agreement as the foundation will help you plan better for all other aspects of the purchase procedure.

The risk of practice owner financing is high.

New Owner financing can be a good choice for the buyer and the dental practice seller. It eliminates the need for a third party to evaluate the asking price and gives the seller more control over their retirement funds. However, it can also be quite risky:

  1. There may be no incentive for either party to negotiate a fair deal since the seller will benefit directly from any increased asking price.
  2. It could leave buyers vulnerable to inaccurate valuations or hidden costs not disclosed by the seller.
  3. There's little recourse if something goes wrong with payments or other key terms of the agreement since no third-party mediator is involved in the sale procedure.

For these reasons, it's often best to secure financing through a bank instead of relying solely on owner-financing when purchasing a dental practice.

Buying a practice or negotiating a lease.

Real estate purchase or lease negotiation is separate from the dental practice acquisition. The property itself plays a critical role in the purchase process, as its ownership or lease terms can significantly impact loan structuring and negotiations. It is also important to determine if the seller is willing to sell the property along with the practice, or if only the practice is being transferred. Always verify which assets or property are being sold as part of the transaction to avoid misunderstandings. It is essential to seek help early in the process and involve your banker, as they are interested in helping you succeed.

Lenders often require that the lease duration be equal to the practice loan term. Therefore, it is crucial to understand the full scope of any potential real estate contracts before signing.

Additionally, it is vital to assess the value and health of the practice before entering into any negotiations for both purchase and lease arrangements. These steps will give you more confidence in securing a deal that meets your financial needs.

Due Diligence in Dental Practice Acquisition

Due diligence is an essential step in the dental practice acquisition process, providing you with a clear picture of the practice you’re about to acquire. Begin by thoroughly reviewing the practice’s financial records, including cash flow statements, tax returns, and accounts receivable. This analysis will help you assess the practice’s financial performance and identify any red flags or opportunities for improvement.

Examine the patient base to understand the demographics, retention rates, and sources of new patients. Evaluate the condition and value of the equipment, ensuring it meets your standards and won’t require immediate replacement. Review all contracts, including those with suppliers, staff, and insurance providers, to uncover any obligations or liabilities that could impact your ownership.

Don’t forget to verify insurance coverage and ensure all legal documents are in order. By conducting comprehensive due diligence, you minimize the risk of unexpected issues and lay the groundwork for a smooth, successful transition into your new dental practice.

A dental attorney understands the unique legal and regulatory challenges of dental practice acquisitions and can guide you through every step of the process. They will review and negotiate contracts, help structure the purchase to protect your interests, and ensure all transaction documents are accurate and complete.

A dental specific attorney can also advise on issues such as real estate purchase or lease agreements, employment contracts, and compliance with state dental board regulations. Their expertise helps you avoid costly mistakes and ensures that the transition is handled efficiently and legally. By working with a dental attorney, you can focus on your new role as a practice owner, confident that the legal aspects of your acquisition are in expert hands.

Final Closing Tasks

The final closing tasks are the last, but crucial, steps in the dental practice acquisition process. At this stage, you’ll complete all necessary paperwork, including signing the purchase agreement and transferring funds. It’s important to update the practice’s records, licenses, and registrations to reflect the new ownership, and to notify the state dental board and insurance companies of the change.

Ensure that all equipment, office supplies, and assets are properly transferred, and that any outstanding financial or legal obligations are settled. Communicate with the practice’s employees to inform them of the transition and address any questions or concerns. By carefully managing these final closing tasks, you’ll pave the way for a smooth transition and be ready to start operating your new dental practice with confidence.

Transitioning to a New Practice

Transitioning to a new practice is an exciting opportunity to build on the foundation of an established dental office. To ensure a successful transition, develop a detailed transition plan that outlines key steps and timelines. Introduce yourself to existing patients and referral sources, emphasizing your commitment to maintaining the same quality of patient care and service.

Update the practice’s marketing materials and online presence to reflect the new ownership, and communicate openly with staff to foster trust and continuity. Focus on building relationships with existing patients and employees, listening to their concerns and feedback throughout the transition process. By prioritizing clear communication, empathy, and transparency, you’ll minimize disruption and set the stage for a smooth transition and long-term success in your new dental practice.

Frequently asked questions

What should I consider before signing a practice purchase agreement?

Before signing a practice purchase agreement, it is important to carefully evaluate all aspects of the sale, including the purchase price, payment terms, liabilities assumed by both parties, warranties or guarantees, and any other conditions included in the document. Additionally, it is important to consult with an attorney or accountant familiar with medical practice acquisitions when drafting a purchase agreement.

What Are Dental Transition Consultants?

Dental transition consultants are experienced professionals who specialize in assisting dentists in the procedure of buying or selling a dental practice. They can advise on all aspects of the transaction, including financial analysis, legal and regulatory considerations, negotiation tactics, closing date, and due diligence.

What are dental practice transitions?

Dental practice transitions involve acquiring, buying, or selling dental practices. The transition can involve negotiations between the buyer and seller, financing arrangements, legal considerations, and other important details necessary to ensure that the purchase or sale takes place smoothly.

Is purchasing an existing dental practice a good investment?

Buying an existing dental practice can be a worthwhile investment when properly evaluated, offering established patient base, cash flow, and operational systems.

Practice acquisitions provide immediate revenue streams and existing infrastructure compared to starting from scratch. Success depends on thorough due diligence, accurate valuation, proper financing, and smooth transition planning. Key factors include patient retention rates, financial performance, equipment condition, and lease terms. Professional legal and financial guidance ensures proper contract terms, regulatory compliance, and protection against hidden liabilities throughout the acquisition process.

How does the 80/20 principle apply to dental practice management?

The 80/20 rule in dentistry suggests that 80% of practice revenue typically comes from 20% of patients, or 80% of problems stem from 20% of issues.

This principle helps practices focus resources on high-value patients, most profitable procedures, or critical operational issues. For example, identifying the 20% of patients generating most revenue allows targeted retention efforts. Similarly, addressing the 20% of processes causing 80% of administrative problems can dramatically improve efficiency. Understanding these patterns helps optimize scheduling, treatment planning, and business development strategies.

What are the typical expenses for acquiring a dental practice?

Dental practice acquisitions typically cost 60-80% of annual gross revenue, plus additional expenses for legal fees, due diligence, and transition costs.

Total acquisition costs include purchase price, legal fees ($5,000-15,000), accounting services, practice appraisal, loan fees, and transition expenses. Buyers should budget for working capital, equipment updates, and potential patient retention investments. Financing options include SBA loans, conventional bank loans, or seller financing. Proper financial planning ensures adequate cash flow for operations while servicing acquisition debt and maintaining practice growth.

Is it possible to purchase a dental practice as a non-dentist investor?

In most states, non-dentists cannot directly own dental practices due to professional practice laws, though some indirect ownership structures may be permitted.

State dental practice acts typically require licensed dentists to maintain ownership and control of dental practices. However, some states allow non-dentist ownership through specific corporate structures or management agreements, subject to strict regulations. Dental Service Organizations (DSOs) provide alternative models for non-dentist investment. Any ownership structure must comply with state professional regulations and avoid unlicensed practice of dentistry violations.

How does the 50-40-30 financial framework guide dental practice operations?

The 50-40-30 rule suggests allocating 50% of revenue to overhead costs, 40% to staff compensation and benefits, and 30% to doctor compensation.

This framework provides benchmarks for healthy financial management, though actual percentages vary by practice type, location, and growth stage. Overhead includes rent, utilities, supplies, and equipment costs. Staff expenses cover salaries, benefits, and training. Doctor compensation includes salary, benefits, and profit distributions. Regular financial analysis using these guidelines helps identify areas needing adjustment and supports sustainable practice growth.

What methods determine dental practice valuation and worth?

Dental practice valuation typically uses asset-based, income-based, or market-based approaches, often combining multiple methods for accurate assessment.

Common methods include percentage of annual gross revenue (60-80%), multiple of adjusted net income, or asset valuation plus goodwill. Professional appraisers consider patient base stability, location, equipment condition, lease terms, and market conditions. Factors like specialty focus, growth trends, and competition affect value. Accurate valuation requires comprehensive financial analysis, market research, and understanding of practice-specific assets and liabilities.

What challenges are involved in acquiring an existing dental practice?

Practice acquisition challenges include securing financing, conducting thorough due diligence, ensuring smooth patient transitions, and navigating complex legal requirements.

Common difficulties involve valuation disagreements, financing approval, hidden liabilities discovery, staff retention issues, and patient transfer complications. Regulatory compliance, license transfers, insurance credentialing, and lease assignments require careful coordination. Technology integration, equipment condition assessment, and contract reviews demand professional expertise. Success requires experienced legal counsel, financial advisors, and comprehensive transition planning to minimize risks and ensure operational continuity.

Is franchising available as an option for dental practice ownership?

Traditional franchising is generally not available for dental practices due to professional practice regulations, though some franchise-like business models exist.

State laws typically prohibit non-dentist control over clinical decisions, limiting true franchise opportunities. However, some companies offer franchise-style support services, branding, and operational systems while maintaining proper dentist ownership. Dental Service Organizations (DSOs) provide similar benefits through management agreements. Any franchise-like arrangement must comply with professional practice acts and avoid unlicensed practice violations while ensuring dentist autonomy over clinical care.

What are the steps to establish or acquire a dental practice?

Establishing a dental practice involves business planning, financing, location selection, licensing, equipment purchase, and staff hiring, while acquisition requires due diligence and purchase negotiations.

New practice establishment includes market analysis, business plan development, financing arrangements, location lease negotiation, equipment purchasing, staff recruitment, and marketing launch. Practice acquisition involves target identification, financial analysis, due diligence, purchase negotiations, financing approval, and transition planning. Both paths require professional legal guidance for regulatory compliance, contract review, and proper business formation to ensure successful practice launch.

What profit margins indicate a healthy and successful dental practice?

Healthy dental practices typically maintain profit margins of 25-40% of gross revenue, though margins vary by practice type, location, and operational efficiency.

General practices often achieve 25-35% margins, while specialty practices may reach 35-45% due to higher procedure values. Key factors affecting profitability include overhead control, production efficiency, insurance mix, and fee schedules. Successful practices balance competitive pricing with operational costs while investing in growth opportunities. Regular financial monitoring, expense management, and revenue optimization strategies help maintain healthy margins and support long-term practice sustainability.
