Legal and Operational Insights for Multi-Location Dental Practices

By Cohen Law Firm Editorial Team. Published January 30, 2025, updated May 25, 2026.

Expanding to multiple locations requires careful consideration of legal structure, operational efficiency, and market dynamics. A thoughtful approach to growth can help protect your existing practice while building a stronger overall business. Here's how to approach expansion strategically and sustainably.

1. Decide on a Multi-Entity or Single-Entity Structure

The legal structure of your multi-location practice impacts everything from liability protection to partnership flexibility. A single-entity structure offers simplified management and accounting, reduced administrative overhead, and streamlined vendor relationships. This approach works well for practices maintaining consistent ownership across all locations and prioritizing operational efficiency.

Alternatively, a multi-entity structure provides enhanced liability protection and flexibility for location-specific partnerships. This model simplifies future practice transitions and allows for precise performance tracking at each location. While it may increase administrative complexity, the benefits often outweigh the costs for practices planning significant expansion or anticipating future ownership changes.

2. Conduct Thorough Due Diligence

Successful expansion requires thorough evaluation of each potential location. Begin with comprehensive demographic analysis of the target market, examining factors like population growth, income levels, and insurance coverage rates. Understanding the competitive landscape, including both traditional practices and DSOs, helps inform site selection and service positioning.

Local staffing availability and costs should factor prominently in your analysis. Even the most promising location will struggle without adequate access to qualified dental professionals and support staff. Consider local regulatory requirements and their impact on operational costs and practice management. Most importantly, develop detailed financial projections to ensure new locations won't drain resources from your existing successful practice.

3. Consider the Competitive Landscape

The growing presence of DSOs has fundamentally changed the competitive landscape for independent practices. Multi-location expansion can help maintain market position by creating economies of scale in equipment and supplies, enhancing bargaining power with insurance providers, and allowing for shared administrative resources. Strategic location selection becomes crucial, as positioning new offices to protect and expand market share can help maintain competitiveness against larger organizations.

Success in multi-location expansion requires careful attention to state regulations regarding dental practice ownership and management structure. Work with experienced legal counsel to ensure your growth strategy complies with all applicable regulations while supporting your long-term goals. This includes understanding restrictions on practice ownership, supervision requirements, and corporate practice of dentistry laws.

Conclusion

Maintaining quality care across all locations remains paramount during expansion. Your growth strategy should include systems for maintaining consistent patient care standards, efficient communication between locations, and effective quality control measures. Consider implementing centralized management systems for key functions like scheduling, billing, and inventory management to improve operational efficiency.

The key to successful expansion lies in careful planning and execution. Each new location should strengthen your overall practice while maintaining the quality of care that built your initial success. By taking a methodical approach to growth and working with experienced advisors, you can build a thriving multi-location practice that serves both patients and practitioners well.
