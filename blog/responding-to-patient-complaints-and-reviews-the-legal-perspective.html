Responding to patient complaints and reviews: the legal perspective

By Dhruva William, Marketing. Published March 24, 2023, updated May 25, 2026.

As adental attorney, one of the most common issues that dental practices face is responding to patient complaints and reviews. These can range from minor grievances to serious accusations of malpractice, and it's essential that dental practices have a plan in place for responding to them. In this article, we'll explore the legal perspective of responding to patient complaints and reviews and provide some tips for managing them effectively.

The Importance of Responding to Patient Complaints and Reviews

Before we dive into the legal aspects of responding to patient complaints and reviews, let's first talk about why it's so important to respond to them in the first place. First and foremost, responding to patient complaints and reviews can help to diffuse the situation and prevent it from escalating. It also shows other patients that you take their concerns seriously and are committed to providing excellent care.

From a legal standpoint, responding to patient complaints and reviews is essential because it can help to protect your practice from potential lawsuits. By addressing patient complaints promptly and professionally, you can demonstrate that you take patient concerns seriously and are committed to providing high-quality care. This can go a long way in preventing a disgruntled patient from filing a malpractice suit or complaint with the dental board.

When responding to patient complaints, there are several important legal considerations to keep in mind. First and foremost, it's important to respond promptly and professionally. This means acknowledging the patient's concerns and offering a solution or explanation for the issue at hand.

It's also essential to keep the response confidential and only share information with those who need to know. For example, if the patient's complaint involves a specific staff member, you may need to discuss the issue with that staff member to resolve it. However, you should avoid sharing any personal or sensitive patient information in your response.

Another important legal consideration when responding to patient complaints is to avoid making any admissions of fault or liability. Even if you believe that the issue was not your fault, it's important to approach the situation with empathy and professionalism. You can acknowledge the patient's experience without admitting fault or liability, which can help to prevent the situation from escalating.

Responding to Patient Reviews

When it comes to responding to patient reviews, the legal considerations are similar to those involved in responding to complaints. However, there are a few additional things to keep in mind.

First and foremost, it's important to maintain patient confidentiality when responding to reviews. You should never disclose any personal or sensitive patient information in your response, even if the reviewer mentions it in their review.

It's also important to avoid making any defamatory or retaliatory comments in your response. This means avoiding any comments that could be perceived as negative or critical of the patient, even if you believe that their review is unfounded or unfair. Instead, focus on addressing their concerns and offering a solution or explanation for the issue at hand.

Finally, it's important to be mindful of HIPAA regulations when responding to patient reviews. This means avoiding any comments that could be perceived as a violation of patient privacy, even if the reviewer has shared their personal information publicly.

Tips for Managing Patient Complaints and Reviews

Now that we've covered the legal considerations involved in responding to patient complaints and reviews, let's talk about some tips for managing them effectively.

First and foremost, it's important to have a plan in place for responding to patient complaints and reviews. This means designating a specific staff member or team to handle these issues and providing them with clear guidelines for how to respond.

It's also important to monitor your online reputation regularly and respond to reviews promptly. This can help to prevent negative reviews from escalating and can demonstrate to other patients that you are committed to addressing patient concerns.

Finally, it's important to maintain open communication with patients throughout their care. By establishing a rapport with patients and addressing any concerns or issues as they arise, you can prevent many complaints and negative reviews from occurring in the first place.

Conclusion

Responding to patient complaints and reviews is an important aspect of running a dental practice, and it's essential to approach these situations with professionalism and empathy. From a legal standpoint, it's important to maintain patient confidentiality, avoid making any admissions of fault or liability, and be mindful of HIPAA regulations.

By having a plan in place for managing patient complaints and reviews, monitoring your online reputation regularly, and maintaining open communication with patients, you can prevent many issues from arising in the first place. And in the event that a patient does file a malpractice suit or complaint with the dental board, you can demonstrate that you took their concerns seriously and made every effort to resolve the issue in a professional and ethical manner.
